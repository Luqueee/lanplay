A7.1, the clock rate audit. What the three measurements came to, and the
arithmetic that connects them, so that none of it has to be reconstructed from
the two envelopes beside this file.

The run is clean-1200s in this directory, taken by
`tools/audio-e2e-gate.sh 90 1200`. The 90 s arm is here too, and
loopback-control.receiver.out is the same receiver driven over 127.0.0.1 with no
radio and no host, which is where the samples identity was checked against a
source with no lateness in it.

  source_ppm  -15.0769 +-0.0001   endpoints -15.0663   125999 readings, 1259.999 s
  sink_ppm     +5.1545 +-0.00003  endpoints  +5.1451   224993 readings, 1199.951 s
  difference  -20.2314 ppm

source_ppm is the capture device's position against the performance counter
`IAudioCaptureClient::GetBuffer` reports for the same frame, on the host and
against the host's own monotonic clock. sink_ppm is `mSampleTime` against
`mHostTime` from one CoreAudio IO cycle's timestamp, on this Mac and against this
Mac's own monotonic clock. Neither figure has ever been subtracted from a reading
taken on the other machine. Both carry a least-squares standard error over every
reading rather than an endpoint difference alone, because an endpoints-only
estimate carries the timestamp noise of exactly two readings and there is no way
to know from two readings what that noise is. It turned out to be 0.31 samples on
the host counter and 0.37 on the host clock here, which is why the errors are
thousandths of a ppm; had it been a millisecond the same window would have
resolved nothing.

  observed growth   +238 +-75 samples over 1199.950 s
  predicted growth  -1165 samples
  gap               +1403 +-75 samples = +24.37 +-1.30 ppm, 18.8 sigma

They do not close and they disagree in sign. The prediction says the buffers empty
by twenty-four milliseconds of audio; they filled by five.

The observed figure is a least-squares line through 120 window snapshots of what
the two buffers held, on a time axis that is the cumulative count of producer
pulls times 5 ms - 239990 periods, every one of them a wait on
`mach_continuous_time` - rather than on summed printed spans, so the axis is an
exact integer count of this Mac's own periods. The run total alone cannot be used
for this: a single snapshot carries about 236 samples of sawtooth, because
`produced` steps by 240 and `consumed` by 256, and the jump distribution across
the run is made of nothing but multiples of those two - 0, 224, 240, 256, 272,
480, 512, 720, 736, 960, 1200.

That scatter is what sets the arm length, and it is the whole of the defence for
1200 seconds:

    300 s    30 windows   growth +- 149 samples   3-sigma floor 31.1 ppm
    600 s    60 windows   growth +- 106 samples   3-sigma floor 11.0 ppm
    900 s    90 windows   growth +-  86 samples   3-sigma floor  6.0 ppm
   1200 s   120 windows   growth +-  75 samples   3-sigma floor  3.9 ppm
   1800 s   180 windows   growth +-  61 samples   3-sigma floor  2.1 ppm

At five minutes the floor is above the twenty ppm under discussion, so the
measurement would have been unable to see its own subject. The 90 s arm proves
that rather than assuming it: its growth is -297 +-482 samples, which contains
the -88 the rates predict and contains zero as well. The two rate figures, by
contrast, resolve in seconds - the tone probe read the output device at +5.229
+-0.004 ppm in sixty seconds - so the rates and the growth demand very different
windows and the growth is what binds.

The identity closed to the sample on every arm, which is what says nothing is
missing from the accounting rather than merely unnoticed:

  loopback control, 60 s   buffer growth 2752 samples held 2752 residual 0
  clean-90s                buffer growth 2048 samples held 2048 residual 0
  clean-1200s              buffer growth 3232 samples held 3232 residual 0

`growth` is eight counters - produced, concealed, lost, late, off grid, oversize,
ceiling discards, decode failures, ring refusals, consumed - and `held` is two
occupancy reads taken after the device stopped. They share no input, so a
disagreement between them would be a term nobody had named. On the long arm the
discards are 3597600 samples against 3596400 concealed, and both are four hundred
times the growth being measured, which is why a version of this arithmetic that
left the late frames out would have been wrong by two orders of magnitude and
inverted in sign.

Where the gap is not. Late 14985 and concealed 14985 are exactly equal, so those
terms cancel to the sample and the only net discard in the run is the ceiling's
5 frames, 1200 samples - close enough to the 1403 gap to be worth excluding
rather than assuming away. It is excluded three ways. The event lies outside the
fitted windows: cursor travel inside them is 239990 frames against 239990 pulls,
zero skips, while the run total is 240005 against 240000. The fit is a line and
not a step: residual means are +2.0 and -2.0 samples across the two halves and
the half-to-half difference in what was held is +115 samples against the +119 the
trend predicts. And the largest single move in the series, +1200 at window 110,
has the wrong sign for a ceiling event, since a skip drains.

Where the gap is. The thread that drains the jitter buffer is paced by neither
audio clock. It pulled exactly 240000 times in exactly 1200 s of
`mach_continuous_time`, on an absolute schedule from the playout anchor, so the
jitter buffer's balance is the host device's rate expressed in THIS machine's
seconds against nominal, and the ring's balance is nominal against the output
device's rate. Only the second of those is `sink_ppm`. The first requires
converting host performance-counter seconds into mach seconds, and that
conversion is exactly what no measurement taken on one machine can make. So

  the two monotonic clocks differ by      -24.367 +-1.297 ppm
  host audio clock in this Mac's seconds   +9.290 ppm, not -15.077
  this Mac's output clock                  +5.155 ppm
  net fill                                 +4.135 ppm

and the buffer fills. Split into the two buffers that predicts +535 samples of
jitter growth and -297 of ring drain, summing to the +238 observed.

The corroboration that survives is the occupancy staircase, and it is
independent because it comes from a histogram of frame counts rather than from any
of the counters above. Its p50 went 0.0, 5.0, 10.0, 15.0 ms with three steps at
windows 15, 56 and 96 - 405 s per 5 ms step, +12.35 ppm, against the +9.29 the
invariant predicts for that buffer alone, agreeing inside the one frame the
instrument is quantised to. Three steps rather than A6's one, which is what makes
this staircase carry a rate where that one carried none.

The corroboration that does not survive, recorded because it was offered before it
was checked: the arrival delay's p50 moved from -10.9 ms on the 90 s arm to -36.3
on the 1200 s one, which looks like drift and cannot be used as one. The deadline
is anchored on the first admitted packet's own arrival plus the target, so a first
packet that turned up in a bad moment shifts every delay in the run by a constant
and permanently; twenty-five milliseconds of anchor offset reproduces the whole
difference with no drift at all. It corroborates the sign and nothing else, and
separating the two would need a per-window arrival delay, which is not recorded.

What both established rows came to. The host endpoint was -15 ppm in A1 and reads
-15.0769 +-0.0001 here. This Mac's output was +5 ppm in A5 and reads +5.1545
+-0.00003, with A5's own render.out corroborating at `measured frames per second
48000.26` and a tone probe taken for this audit reading 48000.25 by that method
and +5.229 +-0.004 ppm by the new one. Neither row is contradicted. What is
contradicted is subtracting them: the difference of two rates taken on two
machines' monotonic clocks is not the rate at which a buffer between them moves,
and the quantity a rate matcher would have to null is the host's audio clock
against this machine's timebase, which is +9.290 ppm and is not measurable on
either machine alone. It is measurable the way it was measured here, from samples
produced against samples consumed, and that is the instrument any correction
should be driven from.

Between the two arms the source moved +0.182 ppm and the sink +0.015 ppm, both far
outside their standard errors, so a crystal's figure is a reading and not a
constant: the sender ran 150 s on the short arm and 1260 s on the long one, and a
warm oscillator is not a cold one. Any correction has to track rather than be
calibrated once.
